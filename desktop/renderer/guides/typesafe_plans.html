The short answer

Use Jev when the answer is a label, a yes/no probability, or a level on a rubric. Let an LLM write, search find evidence, code enforce exact rules, and a human own consequential decisions. Before enabling it for private work, review both TypeSafe's handling of the submitted state and omp's online fallback destinations.

01A decision engine, not a writer

TypeSafe is the provider. Jev is its System One model family. Instead of continuing a chat, Jev evaluates supplied text against explicit questions. Its compact typed answers are meant for software to consume: choose an option, estimate whether a condition holds, or score against ordered descriptions.

Vendor model scope: Models and supported inputs. Generation limits: Jev 1.13 jaggedness.

Results behind a moving alias can change without your workflow changing. Check the version and input limits when evaluating a workload.

03What LUCID actually sends

In LUCID, open Settings > Judgment. The TypeSafe credential is named TYPESAFE_API_KEY. It is separate from your conversational model's credential. Jev is an out-of-band judgment backend, not a replacement selection in the chat-model catalog.

The agent's judge(state, questions) helper supplies the judgment input. State is the material the agent explicitly passes to that call: perhaps an excerpt, search candidates, a code diff, or structured records. Each question carries a type, instructions and criteria. It is not automatically the entire conversation, but the agent can put conversation content, source code or other sensitive material into state. Question text can contain sensitive information too. Smaller does not mean harmless.

Fallback can send the same material elsewhere

After a TypeSafe error that is not an abort, the pinned omp resolver can pass the same state and questions to its online chat judgment chain. It considers configured tiny, smol and default roles, their retry/fallback candidates, and the active session model. Other configured online chat providers may therefore receive the judgment material. Those providers' own terms apply. A TypeSafe ZDR agreement does not cover them.

The helper exposes choice, bool and score. TypeSafe calls the yes/no probability primitive Noul. The request includes state, questions and a model selection. In this pinned client the default destination is https://api.typesafe.ai, the default model is jev-latest, and the advanced environment override names are TYPESAFE_BASE_URL and TYPESAFE_DEFAULT_MODEL. An override is not a residency or retention policy.

Implementation evidence: bundled desktop/judgment_policy.ts, desktop/acp_backend.ts, and pinned omp src/judgment/index.ts plus pi-ai src/judgment/typesafe.ts. Vendor protocol context: TypeSafe models. These LUCID routing facts are implementation-specific, not promises made by TypeSafe.

04Real documented examples, honestly labeled

Evidence boundary: the following are TypeSafe-authored cookbooks and evaluations. They contain worked examples and published outputs, not independently verified customer deployments. Independent production success or customer adoption is not established by these sources. The cookbooks below use jev-1.12, not necessarily the version now behind jev-latest.

Vendor-reported experiment

Rerank legal search results

A BM25 first pass shortlists 30 passages for each of 40 CLERC legal queries from a 3,565-passage corpus. TypeSafe scores each query-candidate pair. The cookbook reports top-1 accuracy rising from 5% to 18% and top-10 from 38% to 62% on that slice. Useful evidence for a retrieval pattern, not a broad legal-research accuracy guarantee; the top result is still often wrong.

Vendor-reported agent experiment

Suggest a skill, with permission to suggest nothing

Two TypeSafe calls first rank a 182-skill Hermes roster, then inspect the top three and allow rejection. Over 488 requests with Claude Haiku 4.5, the cookbook reports wrong-skill loads dropping from 16.8% to 7.3% and needless loads from 9.8% to 4.0%. These are different subsets: covered requests versus requests no skill serves. Covered requests were generated from the skills' own instructions and described as easier than real user requests. This does not establish adoption by Hermes or a LUCID skill-routing feature.

Workflow evaluations are not independent ground truth

TypeSafe's workflow eval site covers security incidents, agent-trace observability, invoice processing and customer service. It compares structured code-plus-judgment workflows with standalone policy prompts. The summary gives the four workflows equal weight and measures agreement with reference labels generated from the average responses of GPT-6 Astra and Claude Fable 5.1 at high thinking; other models use provider-default reasoning settings. The site explicitly assumes the harness is correct.

That methodology can help compare configurations within the vendor's harness. It is not an independently audited production outcome, a human-adjudicated truth set, or proof that Jev should autonomously contain a machine or approve an invoice. The useful pattern is decomposition, not a blanket claim that one model wins every task.

05Typed does not mean trustworthy

  • Injection still matters. TypeSafe says Jev 1.13 does not treat state as hostile by default. Injected instructions, misleading framing and content arguing for its own classification can move its answers.
  • Keep exact work in code. Counting, arithmetic, numerical precision and date ordering are documented weak spots. A score is not an exact quantity reconstructed from prose.
  • Keep the state focused. Irrelevant detail reduces accuracy. Text-only input, bounded context, literal interpretation and multi-hop reasoning limits remain even when output has the correct type.
  • Confidence is not proof. A concentrated choice distribution can still be wrong. Choice probabilities, Noul probabilities and confidence are not interchangeable; thresholds do not automatically transfer across types, questions or versions.
  • Independent questions are not a reasoning conversation. Each is judged against the supplied state. If one decision depends on another, code must enforce that dependency or construct a later call with the needed evidence.

Vendor's explicit failure modes: Jev 1.13 jaggedness, reviewed September 17, 2026. Input/version scope: Models.

Never the sole security decision

Use judgment as an advisory signal, not authorization. Deterministic permissions, trusted policy, input boundaries and required human approval must remain outside the model. A confident answer must not by itself permit a destructive tool, release a secret, bypass lockdown, or declare untrusted content safe. This is LUCID integration guidance, not a vendor safety guarantee.

06Four legal pages, four different jobs

Read scope and operative wording, not just which page is newest. A right to process data is not evidence of how many days a particular system actually retains it. No-training is not zero retention. Website feedback terms are not automatically the API customer-data terms.

Training: a default prohibition, not a universal ban on every use

The Privacy Policy, Services and How We Use sections says it will not train or fine-tune AI/ML models on prompts or other Input. MCA section 4.1 says Customer Data will not enter a dataset used to modify model weights without the customer's prior consent. The model documentation also says Jev is not trained on customer requests or responses and is not per-customer fine-tuned.

These support a no-training default, not an inference that data is never processed, retained or used to derive telemetry. Nor does the newer MCA automatically erase the Privacy Policy. Ask what consent mechanism and signed terms apply to your account if you need an unqualified contractual prohibition.

Processing rights versus actual storage duration

MCA section 4.1 permits processing Input during the term to fulfill the agreement, and Customer Data during the term to provide services and calculate fees. It also grants rights in perpetuity to process Customer Data to derive telemetry, monitor fraud and abuse, and comply with law. That is broad continuing legal permission for those purposes. It does not establish that all raw requests are in fact stored forever, which systems store them, or their expiry times.

MCA section 4.3 defines telemetry to include technical logs, hashes, summary statistics and classifications, metrics and learnings related to service use, and allows processing it without restriction, including improving products. The published definition is broad. It does not demonstrate that telemetry is permanent raw prompts, that hashes are anonymous in every context, or that telemetry is technically undeletable. DPA sections 1.3 and 2 constrain processing where Customer Personal Data is involved.

Deletion is qualified; backups have no stated deadline

The Privacy Policy's Retention section retains personal data as reasonably necessary for services or business/commercial purposes. On request it takes measures to delete or de-identify data when no longer reasonably necessary, unless law requires longer retention. That is not an unconditional erase-now promise.

MCA section 10.3 says TypeSafe need not store Customer Data and may delete it at its discretion, during or after the term. It also allows Customer Confidential Information to remain in standard backups notwithstanding a deletion obligation, subject to confidentiality restrictions. It gives no backup deletion deadline. Permission to delete is not a commitment to delete on a schedule.

DPA Schedule I, item 8 uses a necessity-based period, considering processing purposes and applicable laws including limitation periods. These pages do not publish a single fixed retention period covering requests, responses, operational logs and backups.

Enterprise zero data retention is explicitly offered

The official Legal documentation offers zero data retention (ZDR) for enterprise customers and directs inquiries to privacy@typesafe.ai. This is an enterprise arrangement to confirm and negotiate with TypeSafe, not a LUCID toggle and not an automatic benefit of entering a key. Ask for the contractual scope, exclusions and effective date before sending protected material.

07US hosting is not a US-only guarantee

The Privacy Policy's International Visitors section says the Services are hosted in the United States and describes non-US users transferring personal data to the US for storage and processing. Source for all six entries: TypeSafe Trust Center: subprocessors. The table describes the vendor's published listing, not an independent infrastructure audit.

Do not stretch those labels. They do not establish ownership nationalities, exclusive US personnel access, an end-to-end network route, exact prompt-cache design, or the locations of every backup, log and support access path. “Not stored” on three compute providers is not a service-wide ZDR statement, particularly when AWS has a storage role.

DPA section 6 authorizes international transfers using adequate protections, including EU Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK Addendum. Schedule I describes the transfer frequency as continuous. These are contractual transfer arrangements, not proof of current offshore processing beyond the disclosed regions, and not evidence that the clauses are only about future outsourcing. Neither those clauses nor today's USA labels promise a US-only end-to-end service.

DPA section 3 gives general subprocessor authorization, requires reasonable advance notice for a new subprocessor before access to Customer Personal Data, and allows an objection on reasonable privacy/security grounds within 15 days. Ask how you receive those notices and how objections are resolved for your account.

Ask TypeSafe these exact questions

  1. Retention by data class: What are the normal and maximum retention periods for raw state, questions, responses, request logs, abuse records, support attachments and backups? What creates an exception, and who authorizes it?
  2. Telemetry: Which fields, snippets, hashes and derived classifications are retained? Can they identify us or reconstruct content? What can be excluded, deleted or de-identified, and how does the DPA apply to personal-data-bearing telemetry?
  3. Enterprise ZDR scope: Does it cover request and response bodies, error traces, fraud/abuse monitoring, caches, every subprocessor and backups? Which account, project, endpoint and model are covered, and how do we verify activation?
  4. Deletion completion: What happens after a deletion request and after termination? When do active stores and backups age out, how are legal holds handled, and can you provide written completion confirmation?
  5. Residency and access: Can you contractually restrict processing, storage, backups, logs and support/personnel access to the US? Which locations and legal entities participate today, and which controls enforce the restriction?
  6. Contract hierarchy and consent: Which Order, separate agreement, MCA, Privacy Policy and DPA versions bind our account? How do their no-training provisions interact, and what explicit prior-consent mechanism could permit training?
  7. Subprocessor changes: How do we subscribe to advance notices, exercise the 15-day objection window, and avoid new processing while a reasonable objection remains unresolved?

Use privacy@typesafe.ai for data-handling and enterprise ZDR questions, as directed by the Legal documentation. Keep the initial inquiry free of real prompts, credentials and protected records.

A sensible starting point

Evaluate on public or synthetic material you are allowed to send. Check failure cases, missing answers, adversarial state and your own review thresholds. For private workloads, obtain the handling terms you need and account for every fallback destination before enabling the online route. A useful tiny judgment is still a data disclosure.
