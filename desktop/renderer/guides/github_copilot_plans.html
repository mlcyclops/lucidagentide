1Do this in LUCID right now

The short version

Any Copilot plan, even Free, connects to LUCID with two clicks and a pasted code. If you already pay for Copilot through work or your own GitHub account, this is the fastest way to get GPT, Claude, and Gemini family models into the picker without buying anything new.

Device-code OAuth, step by step

Open Settings > Providers > GitHub Copilot and click Connect. LUCID shows you a short one-time device code and opens the GitHub device-activation page. Paste the code there, approve the sign-in on your GitHub account, and come back: the card flips to connected and the Copilot model catalog appears in the model picker. No API key exists in this flow; the subscription itself is the credential.

GitHub Enterprise accounts

If your organization runs GitHub Enterprise on its own domain, fill the GitHub Enterprise domain field on the card before clicking Connect. Leave it blank for github.com, which is the right value for personal accounts, Free, Pro, Pro+, Max, and most Business seats.

If the connect fails

A failed device-code exchange explains itself with a red toast on the card. The usual causes: the code expired before you pasted it (just click Connect again), or your organization requires an admin to enable Copilot for your seat first.

3AI Credits: the June 2026 billing change

Jun 1, 2026

Every plan moves to dollar-denominated AI Credits. One credit equals exactly $0.01, so an allowance reads like a balance: Pro's allowance, Max's $200, Business's $19/seat. Premium model requests draw the balance down; when it is empty you either wait for the monthly refresh or buy more.

Same date

Code completions become free on every paid plan. The autocomplete stream no longer touches your credits at all. Credits are spent only on premium chat and agent model calls.

Through Aug 2026

Launch promos sweeten the org tiers: Business seats carry $30 in credits instead of $19, Enterprise $70 instead of $39.

Why this pricing model is easy to reason about

1 credit = $0.01 means no opaque request multipliers: you can read a model's burn rate straight off your usage page in dollars. For LUCID agent work, the practical outcome is that heavy sessions spend credits while everyday completions cost nothing extra on any paid plan.

4Why Copilot is special in LUCID

Every other card in Settings connects you to one vendor's models. Copilot is the exception: one subscription surfaces a multi-vendor catalog, with GPT, Claude, and Gemini family models all available through the same Copilot entitlement. For $10 to $39 a month you get to mix frontier families inside a single bill, switch models per task in the picker, and never manage a vendor API key.

GPT family: included Claude family: included Gemini family: included One bill, one OAuth Completions: free on paid plans Premium calls draw AI Credits

Two caveats worth knowing. First, which exact models appear depends on your plan and, on org seats, on what your admin enabled; Max additionally buys priority model access. Second, you are renting these models through GitHub's gateway, so brand-new vendor releases can land on Copilot later than they land on the vendor's own card.

A word on benchmark claims

When you compare the catalog's families, remember that headline SWE-bench Verified numbers are largely self-reported by vendors. Standardized harnesses such as Scale's SWE-bench Pro score every vendor noticeably lower and reshuffle the order. Treat leaderboard deltas of a point or two as noise and pick by trying models on your own work.

The honest comparison against single-vendor plans

A $20 single-vendor subscription buys deeper access to that one family. Copilot Pro at $10 buys shallower but wider access to three. If your work benefits from picking the right family per task, Copilot is the cheapest way to get that breadth; if you live inside one vendor's flagship all day, that vendor's own plan usually serves you better.
