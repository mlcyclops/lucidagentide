1Read this first: the sovereignty gate

Why these cards are hidden behind a typed ACKNOWLEDGE

Every provider in this section either routes your prompts to servers outside the United States or aggregates many origins with no U.S. data-sovereignty guarantee. Your code, your prompts, and your context windows leave U.S. jurisdiction, and no contract in this section promises otherwise. LUCID will not surface these cards until you type the word ACKNOWLEDGE, because this is a decision you should make once, deliberately, and not by accident while clicking through Settings.

Defense and regulated users: treat this entire section as off-limits for sensitive work. Your compliance path in LUCID is the AskSage gov gateway card (org-issued key), which exists exactly so you never need anything on this page.

What the gate is not: a quality judgment. Several models below sit within a point of the frontier on coding benchmarks at a tenth of the price. The gate is purely about where your data goes. If your work is open source, personal, or otherwise fine to leave U.S. jurisdiction, read on; the economics are the best in the industry.

2Do this in LUCID right now

The short version

Type ACKNOWLEDGE in the gated OTHERS section of Settings to reveal the cards. Then it is ordinary key-pasting: each card takes its provider key (the pattern is DEEPSEEK_API_KEY and so on). Two exceptions have real sign-in flows: Qwen offers OAuth through the qwen-portal, and OpenRouter supports OAuth directly in LUCID.

The reveal

Open Settings > Providers and scroll to the gated OTHERS section. Type ACKNOWLEDGE into the confirmation field. The seven provider cards appear and stay revealed for this install.

Keys and the two OAuth exceptions

  • API keys: DeepSeek, Kimi (Moonshot), GLM (Z.AI), MiniMax, and Groq each take a key from the vendor console, pasted into the matching field on their card (DEEPSEEK_API_KEY and equivalents per provider).
  • Qwen: has a real OAuth flow. Click Connect on the Qwen card and sign in through the qwen-portal; no key needed for the subscription path.
  • OpenRouter: supports OAuth in LUCID, or paste a key. One OpenRouter key unlocks many models across vendors, including free variants, which makes it the quickest single setup in this section.

4Benchmarks and cost per point

The headline fact of September 2026: five models cluster at 80.2 to 80.6% on SWE-bench Verified: DeepSeek V4 Pro Max, Gemini 3.1 Pro, MiniMax M3, Qwen3.7 Max, and Kimi K2.6. Three of the five are open-weight models from this page, sitting statistically level with a Western frontier flagship.

Benchmark honesty

SWE-bench Verified numbers are largely self-reported by vendors. Standardized harnesses score everyone lower: on SWE-bench Pro, the best open-weight result is GLM-5.2 at 62.1% (a vendor aggregate figure), and MiniMax M2.7 posts 56.22%. The ordering reshuffles between the two boards, so never compare a self-reported Verified score against someone else's standardized Pro score.

Cost per benchmark point

The most useful lens for this section is not "who is best" but "what does a point cost". Worked example from the standardized board: MiniMax M2.7 scores 56.22% SWE-bench Pro at about a fifth of GLM's cost, while GLM-5.2 leads open weights at 62.1%. That is roughly nine tenths of the leader's score for roughly one fifth of the spend (approximate: prices move and the scored versions trail the priced ones). If your agent runs are high-volume and tolerant of a retry, the cheaper model wins on economics long before the leader wins on quality.

Where the bargains concentrate

Qwen3.7 Flash at $0.03/$0.13 makes experimentation effectively free. MiniMax M3 at $0.30/$1.20 buys an 80%-cluster SWE-V model for pocket change. DeepSeek's cache-hit pricing ($0.007 to 0.014) rewards agent loops that reuse context. These three are the reason this section exists.
